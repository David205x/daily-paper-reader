## Abstract 

Generative retrieval models perform document retrieval by autore-gressively generating document identifiers (DocIDs). This process naturally forms a sequential decision problem, where each decoding step selects a DocID token and the complete token sequence deter-mines the retrieved document. However, retrieval effectiveness is typically evaluated only after the full DocID is generated, creating a mismatch between token-level generation and document-level relevance supervision. As a result, existing reinforcement learning methods for generative retrieval mostly rely on sequence-level re-wards, where the same document-level feedback is propagated to all decoding steps. Such coarse-grained feedback makes it difficult to identify which token decisions are responsible for successful or failed retrieval. In this work, we propose a fine-grained reinforcement learning framework for generative retrieval with token-level relevance re-wards. Instead of assigning a single reward to the entire generated DocID, we estimate step-wise rewards by measuring how each token decision changes the expected retrieval quality of the corre-sponding generation trajectory. This enables more precise credit assignment and encourages the policy to favor token decisions that contribute more directly to document-level relevance. We further develop practical reward estimation strategies tailored to the DocID generation process and incorporate them into a policy optimization framework. ## 1 Introduction 

Information retrieval is a fundamental component of modern in-formation systems, supporting applications such as Web search, open-domain question answering, and retrieval-augmented genera-tion [16, 55]. Recent neural retrievers typically encode queries and documents as dense vectors, retrieve candidates through maximum inner-product search [8, 9], and often apply a separate reranking stage [10]. Although effective, this paradigm depends on an ex-ternal index, fixed-dimensional representations, and multi-stage objectives that do not directly optimize the whole retrieval process toward final relevance [2, 23, 33]. Generative retrieval provides an alternative by directly producing the identifier of a relevant document conditioned on the query [22, 31, 33, 35, 49, 52]. The generated document identifiers (DocIDs) are decoded into retrieved documents, converting retrieval into a sequence generation prob-lem that can represent corpus indexing and query-time retrieval within a unified model. Despite this progress, the training objective of generative re-trieval remains imperfectly aligned with the goal of document retrieval [22, 52]. Most existing methods rely on autoregressive supervised fine-tuning, which maximizes the likelihood of each tar-get DocID token [33, 35, 54]. This objective is natural for sequence generation, but retrieval quality is evaluated only after the com-plete DocID sequence is generated and mapped to a document. The model is therefore optimized at the token level while being judged at the document level. A generated DocID may contain several locally plausible tokens but still refer to an irrelevant document; conversely, some intermediate token decisions may be important because they route the generation process toward a semantically appropriate region of the identifier space. The standard next-token likelihood does not explicitly distinguish these cases, since it treats DocID generation as imitation of observed identifiers rather than direct optimization of document-level relevance. Reinforcement learning provides a principled way to bridge this gap by treating DocID generation as a decision-making process and optimizing the model with relevance feedback [25]. Recent work has explored reinforcement learning from relevance feedback for generative retrieval [52], as well as preference-based optimization methods inspired by direct preference optimization [22]. These approaches move beyond pure teacher forcing and seek to align generated DocIDs with document-level relevance. However, they typically use sequence-level rewards: once a complete DocID is generated, the resulting document receives a relevance score, and the same feedback is assigned to all tokens in the sequence. This coarse supervision leaves a critical credit assignment problem un-resolved [7]. In an autoregressive DocID, different tokens play dif-ferent roles. Early tokens may constrain the candidate subspace, middle tokens may refine semantic or structural choices, and later tokens may determine the exact document. Assigning an identi-cal reward to every decoding step obscures which token decisions actually improve or harm retrieval quality. In this paper, we argue that effective reinforcement learning for generative retrieval requires token-level credit assignment. We for-mulate DocID generation as a sequential decision problem, where the state is the query together with the partially generated DocID, the action is the next DocID token, and the final outcome is the retrieved document. From this perspective, the key challenge is not only whether the generated document is relevant, but also how each token decision changes the expected quality of the generation trajectory. A fine-grained reward should encourage tokens that move the policy toward relevant documents and penalize tokens that divert decoding toward irrelevant regions, even when the final sequence-level outcome alone is ambiguous. To address this challenge, we propose Token-Level Credit Assign-ment for Generative Retrieval (TCA), a fine-grained reinforcement learning framework that separates token-level reward design from the choice of policy optimization algorithm. Our training pipeline follows two stages. First, we perform supervised fine-tuning to endow the model with basic query-to-DocID generation ability. Second, we refine the model with reinforcement learning under token-level credit assignment. Instead of assigning one reward to the entire generated sequence, TCA provides step-wise feed-back for intermediate DocID tokens by comparing the generated hidden-state trajectory with the gold DocID trajectory from a frozen reference model. This trajectory-based reward gives the policy a dense signal for distinguishing helpful and harmful token decisions without requiring an additional reward model. As an optimizer-agnostic credit assignment framework, TCA as-signs fine-grained credit to individual DocID tokens while remain-ing compatible with different reinforcement learning algorithms. In this work, we consider two representative implementations: TCA-GRPO, which uses group-relative advantage estimation to compare multiple candidate DocIDs for the same query, and TCA-PPO, which uses value-function-based advantage estimation under the same token-level reward design. Both implementations are com-patible with different forms of DocIDs and can be combined with constrained decoding over the legal DocID space. We conducted experiments on widely used retrieval benchmarks to evaluate the effectiveness and generality of token-level credit assignment. The results show that TCA consistently improves generative re-trieval performance over supervised fine-tuning and sequence-level reinforcement learning baselines, while the comparison between GRPO and PPO further shows how different advantage estimation strategies affect token-level reinforcement learning for DocID gen-eration. We further analyze different reward designs and training components, demonstrating that fine-grained token rewards pro-vide a robust signal for aligning autoregressive DocID generation with document-level relevance. ## 2 Related Work 2.1 Document Retrieval 

Document retrieval is a fundamental task in information retrieval, where the goal is to find relevant documents from a large corpus given a user query. Traditional approaches to document retrieval include term-based methods such as TF-IDF and BM25 [27], which rely on exact term matching and heuristic scoring functions. More recently, neural retrieval methods have gained popularity, leverag-ing deep learning techniques to learn dense vector representations of queries and documents [10–12, 40, 46]. These methods typically involve encoding queries and documents into a shared vector space and using similarity measures to retrieve relevant documents. How-ever, they often require separate stages for indexing and retrieval, which can be computationally expensive and may not fully capture the complex relationships between queries and documents. 

## 2.2 Generative Retrieval 

Generative retrieval formulates document retrieval as an autore-gressive generation problem, where a model directly generates document identifiers (DocIDs) for a given query. Existing studies have mainly improved this paradigm from four aspects: (i) Iden-tifier construction : Existing DocIDs can be broadly divided into lexical and numerical identifiers. Lexical identifiers, such as titles, URLs [2, 5, 54], representative n-grams, or summaries [1, 14], are human-readable and easy to interpret. Numerical identifiers instead encode documents as discrete semantic codes, including clustering-based semantic codes [33] and RQ-VAE-based quantized identi-fiers [36, 37, 43]. (ii) Index construction : Existing methods enrich indexing through document chunking [33], pseudo-query gener-ation [56], and rehearsal-based augmentation [32]. Other studies further investigate multi-granular indexing [38] and continual in-dexing for dynamic corpora [4, 21, 48, 49]. (iii) Decoding strategy :Existing methods can be broadly divided into autoregressive and non-autoregressive paradigms. Autoregressive decoding generates DocIDs token by token, typically using constrained beam search to restrict the output space to valid DocIDs [2, 17, 26, 33, 44]. Recent studies further explore non-autoregressive DocID generation to improve efficiency and global control [34, 50]. These methods gen-erate multiple DocID tokens in parallel or refine identifiers through iterative procedures, such as diffusion-based language models and parallel generation frameworks. (iv) Training objective : Early gen-erative retrieval models are mainly optimized by supervised likeli-hood training [2, 33, 35]. Later work incorporates ranking-oriented supervision, including marginal ranking loss [18], knowledge dis-tillation [19], and contrastive learning [6, 20]. Recent RL-based methods further optimize retrieval feedback: GenRRL [52] adopts RLHF with an external reward model, while DDRO [22] leverages pairwise relevance feedback without explicit reward modeling. In contrast, our work studies generative retrieval from a sequential decision-making perspective and views DocID generation as a tra-jectory of token-level decisions, where each generated token affects the final document-level retrieval quality. 

## 2.3 Credit Assignment Problem 

Credit assignment concerns how a training signal should be dis-tributed to the individual decisions that produced it. In LLM re-inforcement learning, the central challenge is often token-level: although generation proceeds autoregressively token by token, su-pervision in RLHF is typically provided only as a sequence-level reward on the final response [25]. Some methods introduce denser supervision directly, such as sentence-level human feedback [39], while a larger line of work infers token-level rewards from response-level preference signals through reward densification [3], reward redistribution [15], continuous token-level reward modeling [42], and token-wise preference optimization objectives [41, 45, 51]. Al-though these studies demonstrate the importance of fine-grained credit assignment, they are not directly tailored to generative re-trieval. Unlike open-ended text generation, generative retrieval produces a document identifier as a constrained token sequence, where each token can be viewed as a routing decision over the document space. An incorrect token may lead the decoding trajec-tory to a wrong branch and make the target document unreachable, even if the remaining tokens are locally plausible. Therefore, credit assignment in generative retrieval is more structurally sensitive: the model needs to understand how each token decision contributes to identifying the correct document, rather than simply improving the overall quality of a generated response. This motivates our token-level reinforcement learning framework, which explicitly assigns fine-grained retrieval-oriented rewards to intermediate DocID gen-eration steps and optimizes generative retrieval from a sequential decision-making perspective.

## 4 Method 4.1 Overview 

We propose Token-Level Credit Assignment for Generative Retrieval 

(TCA), a reinforcement learning framework that assigns credit to individual DocID tokens in generative document retrieval. Given a query, a generative retriever autoregressively produces a document Token-Level Credit Assignment Optimization for Generative Document Retrieval CIKM ’26, November 7–11, 2026, Rome, Italy 

identifier, and the generated identifier is mapped back to a document in the corpus. The key idea of our method is to optimize this process at the same granularity at which decisions are made: instead of assigning one sequence-level reward to the complete DocID, we estimate token-level rewards for intermediate decoding steps and use them to update the generation policy. The training pipeline contains two stages. First, we perform su-pervised fine-tuning (SFT) to initialize the model with basic query-to-DocID generation ability. Second, we run reinforcement learning post-training with token-level credit assignment. In this stage, an SFT model is frozen as a reference model, gold DocID trajectories are cached under teacher forcing, and the current policy gener-ates candidate DocIDs under constrained decoding. Token-level rewards are then computed by comparing the generated hidden-state trajectory with the gold trajectory, and group relative policy optimization is used to update the policy. This design combines token-level credit assignment with the stability of group-relative advantage estimation.

Natural Questions Natural Questions(NQ) [13] is a widely applied benchmark for evaluating retrieval models [33, 35]. We use a subset NQ320k that consists of about 320k query-document pairs, where the documents are gathered from Wikipedia pages, and the queries are natural language questions. Note that our study follows the data processing pipeline as described in prior works [22], and obtained approximately 100k documents. 

MS MARCO MS MARCO document dataset is a collection of queries and web pages from Bing search. Similarly to NQ320k dataset, we rank the documents of the MS MARCO dataset by Token-Level Credit Assignment Optimization for Generative Document Retrieval CIKM ’26, November 7–11, 2026, Rome, Italy

We evaluate the models on the queries of the MS MARCO document dev set and retrieval on the sampled document subset as described in prior works [22]. 

## 5.2 Baselines and Metrics 

We compare our method with representative term-based, dense, and generative retrieval baselines. For term-based retrieval, we include BM25 and DocT5Query. For dense retrieval methods, we include DPR [10], ANCE [40], RepBERT [47], and Sentence-T5 [24]. For generative retrieval, we include DSI [33], DSI-QG [56], NCI [35], SEAL [1], Ultron [54], ROGER [53], MINDER [17], and LTRGR[19]. We also compare with GenRRL [52], a reinforcement learning based generative retrieval method. Following prior works, we evaluate retrieval performance using Recall@{1,5,10}, and Mean Reciprocal Rank at 10 (MRR@10), which jointly measure top-ranked retrieval accuracy and ranking quality within the top 10 results. Statistical significance is determined using paired t-tests with a threshold of 

𝑝 < 0.05. 

Note on result sourcing. Since the source code of GenRRL[52] and ROGER [53] is not publicly accessible, their baseline perfor-mance is cited directly from the corresponding original papers. To enable a controlled evaluation against the most relevant prior method, we independently reproduce other baseline methods un-der the same experimental setup and report the reproduced results by reproducing the released code using the dataset configurations adopted in this work. 

## 5.3 DocID settings. 

We evaluate two types of DocIDs: Title+URL (TU) and Product Quantization (PQ) following DDRO [22]. TU uses document titles and URLs as identifiers, preserving lexical information that is useful for web search scenarios. PQ represents documents with discrete semantic codes obtained from quantized document representations, which can better capture semantic information. 

## 5.4 Implementation details 

The SFT model is based on the T5-base pretrained model follow-ing DDRO [22], and we initialize the policy model from the SFT model and use an identical frozen copy as the reference model. Gold hidden-state trajectories are precomputed offline under teacher forc-ing. During reinforcement learning, candidate DocIDs are sampled with trie-based constrained decoding to ensure that generated se-quences remain within the valid DocID space. We train the policy model for 4 epochs with a learning rate of 1 × 10 −6 and a batch size of 256. For TCA-GRPO, unless otherwise specified, the group size is set to 𝐺 = 8. The KL coefficient is set to 𝛽 = 0 for PQ-based DocIDs and 𝛽 = 0.01 for TU-based DocIDs. During evaluation, we use constrained beam search to generate ranked DocID lists. All experiments were conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

## 6.4 Token-Level vs. Sequence-Level Reward (Q3) 

To isolate the effect of reward granularity, we compare the pro-posed token-level trajectory reward with a standard sequence-level reward while keeping the optimizer fixed to GRPO. As shown in Ta-ble 4, the sequence-level reward already improves over baseline-sft ,indicating that reinforcement learning post-training itself is benefi-cial for generative retrieval. However, replacing this coarse sequence-level feedback with token-level credit assignment yields consistent additional gains. On MS MARCO-TU, the token-level reward im-proves R@1 from 39.10 to 39.60 and MRR@10 from 50.86 to 51.10 compared with the sequence-level reward. On NQ-PQ, the gains are larger, with R@1 increasing from 48.80 to 50.02 and MRR@10 from 55.24 to 56.10. These results suggest that assigning the same ter-minal reward to every generated DocID token provides useful but insufficient supervision. By providing position-wise feedback, the token-level reward more precisely reinforces decisions that keep the generation trajectory close to the gold DocID and penalizes deviations from it.

To qualitatively examine how TCA improves DocID generation, we analyze a representative MS MARCO-TU query: “how much does a pharmacy tech make?” The gold document is D702099 , whose TU DocID is “pharmacy technician salaries www.glassdoor.com”. With constrained beam-search inference using 10 beams, the SFT model ranks the DocID only at the 9th position, whereas TCA-GRPO promotes it to rank 1. ## 7 Conclusion 

Generative retrieval generates DocIDs token by token, but existing RL feedback is usually document-level. This paper addresses the mismatch with TCA, which compares generated hidden states with gold DocID trajectories from a frozen reference model to assign token-level trajectory rewards. TCA improves generative retrieval by turning document-level supervision into dense feedback over the actual decoding trajectory. Across MS MARCO and NQ, TCA-GRPO consistently improves top-ranking metrics over supervised fine-tuning and sequence-level reinforcement learning baselines, while TCA-PPO shows that the reward design is not tied to a single optimizer. The reward ablations further indicate that the gains come from token-level credit assignment rather than reinforcement learn-ing alone, and the training analyses show that constrained rollout, moderate group size, and DocID-dependent KL regularization are important for stable and effective optimization. These results also highlight two practical limitations. First, TCA introduces a more complex training pipeline because it relies on a supervised refer-ence model before reinforcement learning optimization. Second, the reinforcement learning stage adds nontrivial training overhead due to rollout generation and token-level reward computation. Sim-plifying this two-stage procedure and improving the efficiency of RL-based optimization remain important directions for future work. More broadly, our findings suggest that assigning credit at the same granularity as autoregressive retrieval decisions is a promising route toward better aligning generative retrievers with retrieval objectives.
